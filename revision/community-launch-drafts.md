설명 방식을 확인하려면 다음 두 단원이 좋습니다.

### Title

I made a free Korean/English textbook on the math behind AI papers and model interpretability

### Body

I kept looking up Jacobians, SVD, and probability notation while reading AI papers, so I put together a textbook with a connected learning path.

It starts with mathematical notation, calculus, linear algebra, and probability, then covers Transformer computations, representations, activation patching, and learning dynamics. I included diagrams, worked examples, exercises with solutions, and Python/PyTorch labs for neural computation and interpretability.

You can read the full Korean or English edition without an account or payment. Each lesson lists prerequisites, so you can enter at a topic you need instead of reading from the beginning.

Two sample lessons:

- [Jacobian](https://leeklim.github.io/ai-math-guide/en/part-1-foundations/M03/M03-11-jacobian/): input/output dimensions, rows and columns, and local linearization.
- [Causal attention](https://leeklim.github.io/ai-math-guide/en/part-2-neural-computation/N05/N05-15-causal-scaled-dot-product-attention/): scores, scaling, masking, softmax, and value-weighted outputs with small matrices and code.

I used AI assistance for drafting, the English edition, and figure creation. I ran executable examples and used automated checks for links, notation formatting, preservation of exercises and solutions, and site builds. I haven't obtained expert peer review.

I'd appreciate reports of mathematical errors or places where the explanation assumes knowledge that the prerequisites don't cover. If you try a lesson, which step was hardest to follow?
