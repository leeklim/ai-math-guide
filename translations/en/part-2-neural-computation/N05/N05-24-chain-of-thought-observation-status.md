# N05-24. Observational status of chain-of-thought

## Why this lesson matters

A step-by-step explanation generated by a model is an output token sequence that can be directly observed. It is readable and sometimes helps solve a problem, but concluding that its sentences faithfully report the internal computation requires separate verification. Interpretability research must keep the gap between `was generated` and `is an actual cause` explicit.

## Learning objectives

- Classify generated CoT as a behavioral observation.
- Distinguish legibility, correctness, and faithfulness.
- Calculate an example in which the same output does not uniquely determine the internal representation.
- Explain how CoT interventions and internal activation interventions ask different questions.
- Distinguish claims supported by CoT from claims requiring additional verification.

## Symbols and terms

| Symbol or term | Common spoken reading | Meaning | Shape and scope |
|---|---|---|---|
| CoT | `chain of thought` | A step-by-step natural-language token sequence generated before the answer | observable output |
| legibility | `legibility` | The extent to which people can read and interpret the expressed content | evaluation property |
| correctness | `correctness` | The extent to which intermediate statements and the final answer agree with facts and rules | evaluation property |
| faithfulness | `faithfulness` | The extent to which the expressed reasoning reflects the model process that produced the answer | causal claim |
| post-hoc rationale | `post-hoc rationale` | A plausible explanation constructed after producing a conclusion | possible behavior |

## Core concept 1. What is directly observed?

If a model generates CoT $c$ and answer $a$ given prompt $x$, what is directly observed is the output behavior

\[
(c,a)\sim p_\theta(c,a\mid x)
\]

You can record tokens, log probabilities, decoding conditions, and answer accuracy. This alone does not automatically provide hidden activations, the features actually used, or causal paths.

When CoT is generated first and the answer follows, the joint probability can be factored as $p_\theta(c\mid x)p_\theta(a\mid x,c)$. This means that the generated $c$ enters the answer's token context. This dependency alone does not establish that each reason written in $c$ accurately explains the internal computation. The conditional influence of natural-language tokens and the accuracy of their statements as a process explanation must be checked separately.

## Core concept 2. Three evaluation dimensions

- Legibility: Can a person understand the text?
- Correctness: Are the written calculations and facts correct?
- Faithfulness: Are the written reasons connected to the model process that produced the answer?

A readable, correct CoT can still have unverified faithfulness. Conversely, awkward wording does not itself mean that the text is unrelated to the internal computation.

## Core concept 3. Non-identifiability from output

In this example, write the hidden state at one position as a row vector and let $\mathbf P\in\mathbb R^{d\times d}$ be an orthogonal matrix. Connect the two representations by the coordinate change $\mathbf h_B=\mathbf h_A\mathbf P$ and also change the unembedding to $\mathbf W_B=\mathbf W_A\mathbf P$. Then you can obtain

\[
\mathbf h_B\mathbf W_B^\top
=\mathbf h_A\mathbf W_A^\top
\]

The hidden coordinates differ, but the logits and generated tokens are the same. This small example shows that output alone cannot uniquely recover the internal coordinate representation. It does not mean that this example alone determines CoT faithfulness in full.

The transposed unembedding is $\mathbf W_B^\top=\mathbf P^\top\mathbf W_A^\top$, so the product becomes $\mathbf h_A\mathbf P\mathbf P^\top\mathbf W_A^\top$. Orthogonality, $\mathbf P\mathbf P^\top=I$, cancels the two coordinate transformations. Invertibility alone does not make the same transpose cancel. For an arbitrary invertible $\mathbf P$, the unembedding must instead be matched as $\mathbf W_A\mathbf P^{-\top}$. Here, $\mathbf P^{-\top}=(\mathbf P^{-1})^\top$ is the transpose of the inverse.

The coordinate swap in the lab expresses the same function in different coordinates. It establishes neither different reasoning algorithms nor that a particular CoT is unfaithful. What it establishes is the limited claim that observed logits cannot uniquely determine a hidden coordinate representation.

The hidden tensors differ, but the logits are exactly equal, and the greedy token IDs are also the same: `[1, 0]`. An identical observed token sequence does not uniquely determine the internal representation description.

## Experiments asking about faithfulness

You can truncate a CoT, change some steps, or paraphrase it and examine how the answer distribution changes. You can also put an answer hint in the prompt and, when the model actually responds to it, check whether the CoT discloses that hint. These experiments measure faithfulness under particular operational definitions; they do not fully read all internal reasoning.

Internal activation patching or circuit interventions address different questions. Do not treat an intervention on natural-language CoT tokens and an intervention on hidden states as the same operation.

Separate the locations in the forward path changed by these two manipulations.

## Connection to model interpretability

CoT is useful black-box behavioral evidence. It provides a starting point for identifying error types, self-correction, prompt sensitivity, and answer dependence. Advancing to a white-box explanation, however, requires connecting activations, gradients, interventions, and controls.

Faithfulness can vary with the model, task, prompt, and decoding. Do not generalize a result from one benchmark to all models and reasoning traces.

## Common misconceptions

### Misconception 1. CoT is internal activations output in English

CoT is itself an autoregressively generated token sequence, not a direct dump of internal states.

### Misconception 2. Correct intermediate calculations must be faithful

Correctness has been checked, but whether those calculations were actually used to generate the answer is a separate question.

### Misconception 3. Since CoT can be unfaithful, it contains no information

You can measure output behavior, error patterns, and responses to interventions. Use it at a limited evidence level.

## Exercises

### 1. Classify the evidence

Is the fact that a model generated `2+2=4` an activation observation or an output observation?

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>It is a behavioral observation of output tokens.</details>

### 2. Evaluation dimensions

The text is fluent, but an intermediate arithmetic step is wrong. Evaluate legibility and correctness separately.

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>Legibility may be high because the text is readable, while correctness is low because the arithmetic is wrong.</details>

### 3. Faithfulness

Does a correct CoT alone establish faithfulness?

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>No. The connection between the process producing the answer and the expressed reasons must be tested through interventions or other checks.</details>

### 4. Non-identifiability

If different hidden representations can produce the same logits, can output alone uniquely recover hidden coordinates?

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>No. Multiple internal representations and parameterizations can produce the same observable output.</details>

### 5. Distinguish interventions

Are deleting one CoT sentence and patching the residual at layer 4 the same manipulation?

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>No. The former changes the subsequent input token context; the latter changes a specified internal activation path.</details>

### 6. Supported claim

Truncating the CoT partway through barely changed the answer probability. What is the safest conclusion?

<details><summary>Show solution</summary>This is evidence that, under the specified task, model, and truncation conditions, the later answer did not strongly depend on the removed CoT suffix. It does not justify generalizing that the entire CoT or all internal reasoning is unnecessary.</details>

## Sources and update boundaries

The setup measuring CoT faithfulness through truncation, perturbation, and hint interventions follows [Measuring Faithfulness in Chain-of-Thought Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.13702). Subsequent research on reasoning models also shows that reported CoT cannot be equated with the internal process, but its numerical findings are model- and task-specific, not general laws.

## Lesson summary

- Generated CoT is a directly observable output token sequence.
- Legibility, correctness, and faithfulness are different evaluation dimensions.
- The same output does not uniquely determine the internal representation.
- CoT interventions and activation interventions change different paths.
- Use CoT as behavioral evidence; an internal causal explanation requires additional verification.
